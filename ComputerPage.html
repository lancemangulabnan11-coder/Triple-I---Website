Computer

Device Information

Computers are machines that process information. Early computers were primarily used to solve mathematical problems, but their capabilities have expanded significantly over time. Today, they can perform complex tasks such as predicting weather patterns, managing communication systems, and controlling advanced machinery like automobiles and robotic surgical tools. Computers have also become more affordable, allowing them to be embedded in everyday appliances, making devices like dryers and rice cookers “smart.” However, computers also have limitations, some of which are theoretical. Despite major advancements in data processing and artificial intelligence (AI), they still lack the ability to think in a fully holistic and human-like way. While computers can imitate human behavior, this imitation cannot fully replace the human element, especially in social interactions.

How to Dispose

Nowadays, the technology have been rapidly evolving it's own landscape, computers had become obsolete at an alarming rate. This surge in electronic waste or e-waste, poses a significant environmental challenge. In disposing a computer is crucial in reducing landfill waste and recovering valuable resources. However, with concerns of people about data security and environmental impact, it's essential to understand the best practices for disposing of old computers. Ways of disposing of computer is: Retailers: Many retailers, includes the electronic stores and department stores, have a program of e-waste recycling. Here, you can drop off your old computers at their stores for it to be recycled. Charitable Organizations: If somehow your computer is still working, you can also donate it. There are many non-profit organizations that are refurbish donated computers and distribute them in schools, libraries, or low-income individuals. Refurbishment: If your computer is old but still has functional or working, it can be refurbished and resold. This involves by replacing worn-out components and reinstalling the operating system. Attempt to sell it: You can try selling on websites like Craigslist, eBay, Mercari, or Facebook Marketplace. All you'll need is the computer's specs and a few pictures. Even if your computer is really old, you may still find a buyer online. Some people specifically look for older, "vintage" computer parts.

How to Recycle

In e-cycling, it's easier than it might seem. There are governments that supported recycling services, charities, and even independent businesses that can take your old electronics. However, they also unlikely to accept any hazardous or dangerous items. There are many ways on how to recycle the old computer such as: Electronics Recyclers: There are many specialized electronics recycling companies that offers services for disposing your computer. They often provide a free or even low-cost pickup options and ensure that the computers are recycled responsibly. Planet Partners program: Free recycling for computers and printers. Many electronics retailers and manufacturers accept laptops for recycling through similar takeback programs, making it easy to recycle used devices. At a City Service Department: Residents of the 59 communities in Cuyahoga County can recycle unwanted computer equipment and peripherals at the city service department throughout the year. See more about computer recycling in your community. Many cities offer year-round collections and some hold seasonal collection events, usually in spring or fall. Do not place your computer in curbside recycling.

Desktop Computer

Device Information

A smartphone is a hand device that combines the functionality of a cellular telephone with a computer and a camera. Smartphones debuted in 1994 with IBM's model and later spread to many other companies such as Apple and Samsung. Initially for calls and email, smartphones now enable internet access, gaming, texting, and photography, along with calls and emails. They transformed the telecommunications sector and shifted consumer behavior.

How to Dispose

Old or damaged mobile phones cannot just be thrown in the bin. This is in part due to the chemicals contained in phone batteries that would be harmful to the environment if released into landfill or water systems. But equally, disposing of your phone this way is a waste of valuable materials. Instead, find an appropriate place to dispose your phone.

How to Recycle

You have two main options when it comes to recycling your old phones: either recycle them directly yourself, or hand them to someone or an organization who can handle the recycling o your behalf. You can also: Check your local curbside recycling, sometimes they accept old electronics. Submit your old phone to your local recycling point. Donate your old phone to a charity shop, they will be able to earn money from recycling the raw materials. Donate to a school or a community group. Return your old phone to its manufacturer, who can recycle the parts into new devices.
