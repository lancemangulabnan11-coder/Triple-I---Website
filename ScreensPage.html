Screens

Information

Screens are the display panels used to show visual content and data. They are classified as electronic waste (e-waste) when they become damaged, unusable, obsolete, or discarded. Common types include:

• Display panels from laptops and notebooks

• Tablet screens

• Mobile phone displays

• Screens used in cameras, handheld devices, and appliances

• Separate display panels removed from larger equipment

They contain valuable materials such as glass, plastic coatings, and thin metal layers, but also hazardous substances including liquid crystals, mercury (in older versions), lead solder, and flame retardants. Improper handling can lead to environmental contamination and health risks like skin irritation, respiratory problems, or long-term toxic exposure.

Includes television screens, laptop screens, tablet displays, mobile phone screens, and display panels.

According to Globe Telecom (2025), screen e-waste is classified under the category "Screens and monitors" and contains hazardous materials such as flame retardants, lead, and other heavy metals that can contaminate air, soil, and water if disposed incorrectly. It is also identified as one of the main types of electronic waste generated in the country. These items can be handled responsibly by dropping them off at certified recycling facilities, trading them in through official programs, or donating them if they are still functional. Before disposal, users are advised to delete all personal data to protect their privacy.

From ecyclingcentral.com (2026), screens are accepted at various collection points, retailer take-back programs, and government-organized collection events across Manila. While smaller screen devices are usually accepted free of charge, larger units may require payment for disposal services. These items are covered under Republic Act 9003, also known as the Ecological Solid Waste Management Act of 2000, which sets requirements for proper waste handling and recycling practices.

Based on the information from SM Cyberzone (2025), screen e-waste is accepted in dedicated collection boxes placed in 68 Cyberzone branches and 20 other SM malls nationwide. It is also included in the "Trash to Cash" program, where people can exchange these items for money. These efforts are part of broader sustainability initiatives designed to prevent harmful substances from entering the environment.

Research documented by Semantic Scholar (2024) groups these items as "Screens, monitors, and equipment containing screens having a surface greater than 100 cm²." In local junkshops, complete units are priced between ₱20 and ₱120, while incomplete units are sold by weight—at ₱8 per kilogram for CRT screens and ₱2 per kilogram for HD screens. Processing these items produces residual waste such as glass, plastic casings, and back plastics, which are often difficult to recycle. Among different types of electronic waste, screens rank third in economic value; CRT screens are more valuable because of their leaded glass and copper content, while HD screens are less valuable as they are mostly made of plastic and glass.

Guidance from ABS-CBN (2024) states that screens should be stored properly first, and then users should coordinate with their local barangay or the Department of Environment and Natural Resources for safe and legal disposal. These items are classified as hazardous waste and should never be mixed with regular household trash.

As outlined in the document from the Senate of the Philippines (2018), screens are covered under regulations that require manufacturers to create systems for collecting and recovering products that include display screens. These policies are established to ensure that these items are processed in ways that protect the environment

How to Dispose

If you no longer need your old screens, here are the proper ways to handle them in the Philippines:
Donate or resell
If the screen is still functional or repairable, you can donate it to technical schools or training centers where it can be used for educational purposes. You may also sell it to parts dealers or repair shops that reuse working components.

Participate in collection drives
Join e-waste collection events organized by groups such as UP Circuit’s The E-Waste Project, which regularly holds drives and has designated drop-off points. SM Supermalls also accepts screens through their recycling programs.

Use take-back programs
Brands like Apple, Samsung, and other electronics manufacturers accept old or broken screens through their service centers for safe processing. Telecommunication companies including Globe and Smart also have collection channels nationwide.

Contact local authorities
As advised by the Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR), store screens in a dry and safe place temporarily, then coordinate with your Barangay officials. They will arrange for proper collection and transfer to authorized facilities.

How to Recycle

Recycling screens helps recover usable materials and prevents harmful substances from entering the environment. Follow these steps:

How to Recycle

• Prepare the items: If the screen is cracked or broken, wrap it carefully in paper or cloth to prevent cuts or the spread of hazardous particles. Remove any attached accessories, cables, or small parts, and clean off dust and dirt.

• Choose an accredited facility: Bring your items to DENR-accredited recyclers or organizations like the Philippine Association of Electronic Recyclers Inc. (PAERI), which follow strict safety and environmental standards.

• Drop off or arrange collection: You can drop off screens at designated collection points such as those found in SM Cyberzone branches, or bring them directly to recycling facilities. Small to medium-sized screens are usually accepted free of charge.

• Ensure proper processing: Certified recyclers safely dismantle the units, separate valuable materials, and neutralize or dispose of hazardous components properly.

Additional Information & Options
• Republic Act No. 9003 (Ecological Solid Waste Management Act of 2000): Mandates proper segregation, storage, and disposal of all types of waste, including e-waste. It strictly prohibits mixing hazardous materials found in screens with regular household trash.

• Proposed Legislation: Bills have been filed to establish "manufacturer responsibility," requiring electronics companies to provide free collection and recycling services for the products they sell.

Accredited Recycling Facilities & Programs
• HMR Envirocycle: A DENR-accredited Treatment, Storage and Disposal (TSD) facility that accepts all types of display panels. Locations: Calamba City and Mandaluyong City. Contact: 0917 834 9596

• SM Supermalls E-Waste Collection Program: Has dedicated drop boxes in 68 Cyberzone branches and 20 other malls nationwide, in partnership with PLDT and Smart Communications.

• The E-Waste Project: Accepts screens at collection drives and permanent drop-off points located at UP Diliman.

Economic Value

• Laptop/Tablet Screen (Damaged/Broken): ₱1 – ₱3 per piece
